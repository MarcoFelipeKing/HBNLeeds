What Martín does in the network

Martín is one of three directors who set the network's direction, decide what it funds and keep it honest about whether the research is reaching anyone outside the university.

His own discipline is the one most people are surprised to find in a buildings network. He develops stochastic models of how bacteria and viruses move between people in enclosed spaces, including hospital wards and public transport. The practical value is that a model lets you test a change, say a different ventilation rate or a different occupancy pattern, before anyone spends money building it. That is the bridge he brings: a way of putting numbers on decisions that are otherwise made on intuition.

The research behind it

His published work sits where mathematical epidemiology meets the built environment. Two of the papers below are joint work with Marco-Felipe King, which is roughly how this network started.

Selected work

  • The Wells-Riley model revisited II: parameter uncertainty and population heterogeneity

    Risk Analysis, 2026. With Marco-Felipe King and Catherine Noakes.

    The Wells-Riley equation is the standard tool for estimating airborne infection risk in a room. This paper asks how far you should trust it once the uncertainty in its inputs, and the variation between people, are taken seriously.

  • A quantitative microbial risk assessment approach to estimate exposure to SARS-CoV-2 on a bus

    Journal of Transport and Health, 2024. With Marco-Felipe King.

    Puts numbers on exposure inside a real enclosed space, and shows how ventilation and how many people are aboard change the answer.

  • The reproduction number and its probability distribution for stochastic viral dynamics

    Journal of the Royal Society Interface, 2024.

    The underlying theory. Treating the reproduction number as a distribution rather than a single number is what makes the applied work defensible.

  • Mechanistic within-host mathematical model of inhalational anthrax

    PLoS Computational Biology, 2025.

    The same machinery turned on a single body rather than a room, which is where the dose and response assumptions used in risk assessment come from.

Hundred words

Martín López-García is Professor of Mathematical Biology at the University of Leeds and a co-founder and director of the Healthy Buildings Network Leeds. His research develops stochastic models of infection transmission in indoor environments, including hospitals and public transport, so that interventions can be tested before they are built. He co-founded the network in 2024 to connect quantitative modelling with the people who design, run and live in buildings. He co-edited the UKIEG 2025 Book of Abstracts and chairs sessions across the network's seminar and showcase programme.
