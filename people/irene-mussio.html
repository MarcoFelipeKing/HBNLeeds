What Irene does in the network

Irene is one of three directors who set the network's direction, decide what it funds and keep it honest about whether the research is reaching anyone outside the university.

Her discipline is the one that keeps the network pointed at people. A building only delivers health if somebody opens the window, runs the fan, accepts the retrofit or trusts the reading on the monitor. Irene studies how people perceive risk and what they will give up to reduce it, and she tests it with experiments rather than assuming it. That is the difference between a ventilation strategy that works on paper and one that works in an actual house.

The research behind it

Her work is on how people perceive and respond to risks to their health and their environment, using experimental and behavioural methods. Current projects look at how environmental hazards shape behaviour and risk perception, how preferences shift under different health conditions, and what happens when mindfulness techniques are brought into economic experiments.

Hundred words

Irene Mussio is Lecturer in Economics at Leeds University Business School and a co-founder and director of the Healthy Buildings Network Leeds. Her research spans behavioural, environmental and health economics, with a focus on the economics of risk and uncertainty and on economic valuation. She uses experiments to study how people perceive environmental and health hazards and what they are willing to trade to reduce them, work that connects human decision-making with building design and public health. She co-founded the network in 2024 and co-edited the UKIEG 2025 Book of Abstracts.
