Welcome.

The Healthy Buildings Network (HBN) at the University of Leeds is a cross-disciplinary research and collaboration hub focused on healthier, more sustainable built environments. Founded in 2024 as a Horizons Challenge Network, HBN now spans more than 20 researchers from over 11 departments across the university, alongside partners in local government, industry, and the voluntary sector.

This Showcase marks two years of funded research, community engagement, and interdisciplinary collaboration. Today you will hear from the eight project teams we have supported through our pump-priming and early-career researcher funding programmes, and from leading voices in the broader UK healthy buildings research landscape.

We hope the day sparks new conversations, new partnerships, and new questions.

About the Network
Building bridges across disciplines, councils, and industries
Our Vision
"A world where all buildings enhance the health, wellbeing, and sustainability of their occupants and the planet through collaborative, evidence-based approaches."
Our Mission
HBN emerged to foster meaningful collaboration on how buildings impact health. We actively engage across campuses, councils, and industries — driving innovative research for healthier indoor environments.
Our Funding
Established in 2024 through the University of Leeds Horizons Challenge Network programme. We have delivered two competitive funding rounds — four pump-priming grants (£3,000 each) and four ECR-led grants (£1,500 each, co-funded with the School of Civil Engineering).
Network Reach
Members span engineering, mathematics, business, public health, architecture, environmental science, social science, and the arts — united by a shared commitment to healthier places.

Ucci
UCL Institute for Environmental Design & Engineering
The Bartlett, University College London
Marcella Ucci is a Professor at UCL's Institute for Environmental Design and Engineering (IEDE) at The Bartlett. Her research spans indoor environmental quality, ventilation, building-related health, and the interplay between building design and occupant wellbeing.
She has contributed to major interdisciplinary projects linking energy efficiency with health outcomes in buildings and has advised on national guidance around indoor air quality and healthy building design.
Research Interests
Indoor Air Quality Ventilation Building & Health Energy & Wellbeing Occupant Behaviour

Project 1 · Pump-Priming
Living in Clover
Prof. Gleb Yakubov
Exploring clover as a sustainable living roof option. Beyond insulation and carbon capture, the team investigates nitrogen-fixing qualities to support pollinators, boost soil health, and create multifunctional rooftop spaces that enhance urban biodiversity and circular-economy principles.
Green Roofs Biodiversity Circular Economy
2
Project 2 · Pump-Priming
ThermoAge
Dr Sally Shahzad
Examining how indoor temperature extremes affect posture, muscle function, and fall risk in older adults. Combining controlled climate chamber sessions, biomechanical monitoring, and occupant diaries to identify threshold temperatures where balance and safety are significantly compromised.
Thermal Comfort Ageing Biomechanics
3
Project 3 · Pump-Priming
Human-Centred Housing: Evidence-Based Design
Dr Alexa Ruppertsberg
Bridging the gap between compliance-focused housing standards and the real-world needs of residents. Integrating technical regulations with Post-Occupancy Evaluation data to embed occupant wellbeing, health, and happiness into the design process.
Housing Design Post-Occupancy Wellbeing
4
Project 4 · Pump-Priming
Indoor–Outdoor Air: Passive Houses Study
Prof. Alison Tomlin & Dr Douglas Booker
Investigating how airtight Passive House designs influence indoor and outdoor air quality. Monitoring pollutants including NOₓ, CO₂, PM₂.₅, and ultrafine particles — correlating them with occupant behaviour and ventilation practices.
Air Quality Passive House Ventilation

HBN Showcase 20268
Panel Discussion
8 Questions for the
Future of Healthy Buildings
Curated from over thirty submissions by registered attendees · June 2026
01
The Translation Gap
We already have strong evidence linking indoor environments to health, yet adoption remains slow. What are the biggest barriers — and how do we accelerate translation into real-world practice?
02
Health Equity
How can we design buildings that actively reduce health inequalities — and how do we co-produce healthy buildings with the communities most affected by poor-quality housing?
03
Standards & Measurement
What would it take to create a nationally consistent "healthy buildings score"? What percentage of UK buildings fall below a healthy standard — and what is the cost of inaction?
04
Everyday Air Quality
How should a typical householder know when their indoor air is worse than outdoors? What does genuinely useful, consistent public messaging around ventilation and air quality look like?
05
Affordability & Retrofit
What are the most cost-effective interventions for making buildings healthier? How do we make clean heating genuinely affordable for households in fuel poverty — and persuade landlords to act?
06
Ageing & Diverse Needs
How do we design healthy buildings for older people who want to age in place — and for those with multiple intersecting health conditions? Should "one-size-fits-all" standards even exist?
07
People & Planet
How can we reconcile the competing demands of energy efficiency, climate resilience, and indoor air quality in future building standards — and make buildings genuinely healthy for people and planet?
08
Technology Adoption
Real-time IAQ monitoring, smart ventilation, and AI-driven building controls already exist. What needs to happen — across industry, policy, and research — to accelerate their adoption?

School of Mathematics
Martín focuses on stochastic models for bacteria spread in hospitals, bringing a mathematical and epidemiological perspective to healthy buildings research. He co-founded HBN to bridge quantitative modelling with real-world built environment challenges.
Co-Founder & Director
Dr Irene
Mussio
Leeds University Business School
Irene specialises in behavioural economics and risk-risk trade-offs, investigating how people perceive and respond to environmental risks in buildings. Her work connects human decision-making with building design and public health.
Co-Founder & Director
Dr Marco-Felipe
King
School of Civil Engineering
Marco-Felipe's expertise covers modelling human behaviour in the built environment, including airflow, infection risk, and occupant movement. His research informs how buildings can be designed to protect and promote occupant health.
