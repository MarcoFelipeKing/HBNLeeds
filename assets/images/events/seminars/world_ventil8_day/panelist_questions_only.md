# Questions for the panelists

1. We do already have safe limits for each IAQ component. However, presumably it is about how we understand them in combination? So CO2 may be very low as ventilation is great, yet indoor air quality is terrible as outdoor air quality is terrible, or very high yet pretty good due to effective filtration. One of the embedded myths we need to shatter is surely that ventilation = clean IAQ.

2. I am astonished (also biased!) that respiratory infections are not figuring more in the discussion (COVID is still here, by the way, along with our seasonal viruses, etc.), but I am also confused as to why opening windows is set against filtration/source control. Why not produce an integrated plan and associated guidance to mitigate different challenges in different settings?

3. Are we at risk of creating a system where homes and schools are expected to meet CO2 targets but lack the funding, authority, or infrastructure to achieve them? How can we ensure that air quality standards drive meaningful improvements rather than simply documenting inequalities that schools, homes, and occupants themselves cannot fix, leaving people powerless?

4. Are we becoming too reliant on MVHR as the default solution for airtight buildings? It introduces additional risks around duct hygiene, filtration, commissioning, and long-term maintenance. Should we not be assessing which ventilation strategy is most appropriate for the building and its occupants, and then testing actual indoor air quality, rather than assuming a mechanical system guarantees a healthy outcome?
